Our approach

More than a diagnosis. A clearer pathway forward.

People’s needs rarely fit into one category. STN’s whole-person model considers mental health, physical wellbeing, relationships, environment, culture, safety and practical circumstances together.

The whole-person model

Every part of a person’s life, considered together.

Our approach looks at the wider circumstances that shape wellbeing, not only the presenting concern.

01

Psychological

Thoughts, mood, perception and mental-health history.

02

Emotional

How the person feels and copes day to day.

03

Behavioural

Patterns, habits, substance use and risk.

04

Physical

Physical health, sleep, energy and wellbeing.

05

Social

Relationships, family, whānau and community connection.

06

Environmental

Housing, safety and essential needs.

07

Functional

Work, study, parenting, self-care and daily living.

How we work

Five commitments in every pathway.

Whatever brings someone to STN, the pathway is built around these commitments.

  • Listen to the person’s priorities and lived experience.
  • Understand strengths, needs, risks and protective factors.
  • Coordinate appropriate assessment and therapeutic input.
  • Connect care planning with practical and community supports.
  • Review progress and adapt the pathway as needs change.
From first contact to recovery

A pathway that stays connected.

  1. First contact and consentClear information about what happens next and how information is used.
  2. Whole-person assessmentA structured look at needs, strengths, safety and circumstances.
  3. Shared understandingA clinical formulation that makes sense of the whole picture.
  4. Coordinated supportTherapeutic, practical and community supports working together.
  5. Review and recoveryProgress is reviewed and the plan adapts over time.
Technology with human oversight

Digital tools that support people, not replace them.

STN’s platform is designed to make assessment consistent and information easier to act on, while every clinical decision stays with a person.

  • Information is gathered consistently and securely.
  • A clinician reviews findings before they inform care.
  • Safety concerns are escalated to a person, not handled by software alone.
  • Records are designed to be protected, access-controlled and auditable.
